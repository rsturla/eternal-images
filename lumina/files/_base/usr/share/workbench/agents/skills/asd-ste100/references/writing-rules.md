# STE writing rules

This is a paraphrase of the ASD-STE100 writing rules, grouped by topic. It is a
working summary, not the official text. For the authoritative rules, see
https://asd-ste100.org.

## Contents

- Words
- Noun phrases
- Verbs
- Sentences
- Procedures
- Descriptions
- Warnings, cautions, and notes
- Punctuation and numbers

## Words

- Use only approved words. Use each word in its approved part of speech and its
  approved meaning. See [word-choice.md](word-choice.md).
- Use technical names as needed. Use technical verbs as needed.
- Do not use a word as more than one part of speech.
- Keep to one meaning for one word.
- Do not use slang, jargon, or idioms.
- Do not make new words or short forms without a definition.

## Noun phrases

- Do not use more than three nouns together in a noun cluster.
- Add hyphens or rewrite the phrase to make the relation clear.
- Example: rewrite "runtime configuration file backup" as "backup of the
  runtime configuration file".

## Verbs

- Use only these verb forms: the infinitive, the imperative, the simple
  present, the simple past, the simple future, and the past participle as an
  adjective.
- Do not use the `-ing` form as a verb.
- Do not use compound or complex tenses.
- Use the active voice. Use the passive voice only in a description when the
  agent is not known. Never use the passive voice in a procedure.
- Do not use "shall" or "should" for an instruction. Use the imperative.

## Sentences

- Keep procedure sentences to 20 words or fewer.
- Keep descriptive sentences to 25 words or fewer.
- Write one idea per sentence.
- Do not omit words to make a sentence short. Keep articles and other words
  that aid clarity.
- Connect only related ideas. Do not build long chains of clauses.

## Procedures

- Write one instruction per sentence.
- Start an instruction with a verb in the imperative.
- Put the condition or the purpose before the instruction.
- Use a vertical list for a set of steps or items.
- Number the steps when the order is important.

## Descriptions

- Keep a paragraph to one topic. Use six sentences or fewer.
- Use a clear first sentence that states the topic.
- Use the present tense for a general fact.

## Warnings, cautions, and notes

- Write a warning or a caution before the step it applies to.
- State the condition first. Then state the action to take.
- Make a warning specific. Say what the hazard is and how to avoid it.

## Punctuation and numbers

- Use simple punctuation. Do not use a semicolon. Use two sentences instead.
- Use a hyphen to connect words when it makes the meaning clear. Example:
  "read-only file".
- Do not use a slash ("/") to mean "and" or "or". Write the word instead.
  Example: write "start or stop", not "start/stop".
- Use a colon to introduce a list.
- Use parentheses only for a short reference or an abbreviation. Do not put a
  full sentence in parentheses.
- Use quotation marks for text the reader types or sees on screen.
- Use numerals for numbers, for example "3" not "three".
- Use a consistent format for units, dates, and times. Put a space between a
  number and its unit. Example: "512 MiB".
